# Archetype D (Third-Party Aggregator / Pay-for-Performance / NMEC) — 51-Jurisdiction Screen

**Verified on:** 2026-09-22
**Scope:** Commercial customers only. Energy Plus measures: compressor-cycling optimizer, CryoGenX4 refrigerant-side additive, high-efficiency RTU replacement, solar thermal, thermal barrier.
**Companion file:** `archetype-d-screen.csv` (51 rows, one per state + DC, alphabetical by abbreviation)

## 1. Method

This screen was built by five parallel research passes (California in depth; Northeast/Mid-Atlantic; Midwest; South; West-minus-CA), each instructed to use WebSearch/WebFetch against primary sources only — PUC/PSC dockets, program-administrator sites (NYSERDA, NJ Clean Energy, Mass Save, Energy Trust of Oregon, Focus on Energy, ComEd/Ameren, Xcel, CPUC's NMEC/third-party pages, BPA/NEEA, TVA) — and to record `not_found` rather than guess when nothing turned up.

**A material complication arose mid-screen:** the session's shared WebSearch quota (200 calls) was exhausted partway through the regional passes (Midwest, South, and West agents all hit this independently), and a system-wide concurrent-agent cap blocked further sub-delegation. As a result, roughly 20 of the 51 jurisdictions received **zero or near-zero actual verification** (mostly 404/403s on guessed URLs, no working search) before their agent's budget ran out. Per explicit instruction, those jurisdictions are recorded as `d_exists = not_screened` in the CSV — **not** `not_found` — because "not found after a real search" and "never actually screened" are different claims, and conflating them would misrepresent the reliability of this screen. A "not_found" record means a genuine primary-source search was attempted and came up empty or inconclusive; a "not_screened" record means the state was not meaningfully investigated this round.

The orchestrating session then personally completed direct primary-source research (own WebSearch/WebFetch budget) on the nine most under-researched Northeast/Mid-Atlantic states (VT, NH, ME, PA, MD, DE, DC, VA, WV) plus two coordinator-flagged follow-ups (MA's 2025-2027 plan, NY's post-Business-Energy-Pro status), and ran supplementary spot-checks on several flagged high-value leads in other regions (MN, CO, WA/BPA, CA's Peak Energy question) once regional agents' budgets were exhausted.

**Bottom line on data quality:** treat `yes` and `pilot` rows as reasonably solid (multiple live primary sources fetched). Treat `no` rows as sourced-but-not-exhaustive (typically 1-3 sources per state). Treat `not_found` rows as genuinely searched but inconclusive. Treat `not_screened` rows as an open research backlog, not a finding — see Section 4.

## 2. Ranked list — where Archetype D is strongest for Energy Plus's measures

1. **California (CA) — yes.** The only state with a live, currently-operating, non-utility-implementer measured-savings program confirmed by direct fetch: SoCalGas's GRID-MAP (implementer: Mendota Group LLC) and Service RCx+ (implementer: Veolia North America). The broader PG&E/SCE/SDG&E "Market Access Program" (population-NMEC aggregator marketplace) is CPUC-mandated to continue statewide from July 2024 onward, though current branded enrollment pages could not be pinned down and SDG&E's original emergency tranche formally closed June 2025. NMEC is inherently measure-agnostic — the best fit for CryoGenX4 and the optimizer of any jurisdiction screened.
2. **District of Columbia (DC) — yes.** DCSEU's Pay for Performance program: a genuine non-utility implementer (VEIC) operating under a performance contract with DOEE, paying commercial/institutional buildings (>50,000 sq ft) on verified pre/post metered savings (>10% threshold, 100,000 kWh / 500 MMBtu minimum). Measure-agnostic by design. Realistic entry: act as the customer's ESCO/engineering partner submitting the P4P proposal.
3. **Pennsylvania (PA) — pilot.** Act 129 Phase V uses PUC-registered, non-utility "Conservation Service Providers" (CSPs) selected competitively by each EDC, with custom-measure savings calculated at the meter. The open question — not resolved this session — is whether CSP compensation is tied to ongoing measured savings or is a fixed program-delivery contract; this determines whether PA is a true match.
4. **Texas (TX) — pilot.** The Commercial Standard Offer Program (CSOP) is a genuine open-enrollment, non-utility "service provider" model (TDUs are barred from delivering EE directly), with a Full M&V track for non-standard measures. Payment can go to the applicant/service provider. Primary weakness: mostly deemed $/kWh pricing, not ongoing NMEC.
5. **New York (NY) — no (historical pilot only).** NYSERDA/Con Edison's Business Energy Pro (2019-2022) was a near-perfect match — competitively-selected "Portfolio Managers" paid on CalTRACK/NMEC savings — but appears discontinued with no confirmed 2023-2026 successor despite direct searching. Worth a dedicated follow-up given Con Edison's large new 2026-2030 EE/BE portfolio (PSC order, May 2025).
6. **Washington (WA) — no, but structurally close.** PSE and Avista both run "Pay for Performance" programs with genuine site-NMEC M&V and whole-building, measure-agnostic scope — but both pay the customer directly rather than a non-utility aggregator, so they fail the core archetype-D test. BPA's Energy Smart Industrial program (delivered by third-party Cascade Energy) is a related but industrial-only structure whose payment mechanics to Cascade were not confirmed.
7. **Oregon (OR) — no, but structurally adjacent.** Energy Trust of Oregon is itself a non-utility, ratepayer-funded administrator (same family as DCSEU), but its commercial Custom Upgrades track pays deemed/estimated savings to the customer, not metered savings to an implementer.
8. **Hawaii (HI) — no, but structurally adjacent.** Hawaii Energy is a non-utility, PUC-designated administrator with an explicitly measure-agnostic "design your own project" custom track — but it's a one-time deemed/projected-savings rebate to the customer, not ongoing NMEC paid to an implementer.
9. **Minnesota (MN) — not_found, but flagged.** A 2020 MN Dept. of Commerce feasibility study explored a "Commercial Whole-Building Pay for Performance" concept using population NMEC. Two rounds of searching in 2026 found no live, branded program under that name — only Xcel's standard deemed "Process and Commercial Efficiency" track survives. Whether the 2020 concept was ever adopted is unconfirmed and is the single highest-value unresolved lead outside the Northeast.
10. **New Jersey (NJ) — no, but name-confusing.** NJ's own "Pay for Performance" program is real and active, but pays the customer against modeled/target whole-building savings via a network of pre-approved "Program Partners" — not an implementer paid on metered performance. Useful mainly as a channel-partner (Program Partner) relationship, not archetype D.

**Note on the ~20 `not_screened` states:** several likely low-probability candidates (deep-South and Great Plains states with minimal formal EE portfolios) are in this bucket for lack of research time, not because they were checked and found empty. Section 4 lists follow-up priority.

## 3. California — in depth

California is the fullest expression of archetype D, per the client's own reference case, and merited the deepest research pass.

**Regulatory foundation.** CPUC Decision D.16-08-019 (modified by D.18-01-004) required IOUs, RENs, and CCAs to deliver a portion of efficiency savings through competitively-solicited third-party implementers. A parallel rulemaking, R.13-11-005, produced the "Rulebook for Programs and Projects Based on Normalized Metered Energy Consumption" (NMEC), now at v2.1 (filed November 2023). The Rulebook defines site-level NMEC (individual building vs. its own pre-intervention baseline — closest to IPMVP Option C) and population-level NMEC (a cohort measured and normalized as a group, used for aggregator-marketplace programs), and requires a bid M&V plan from any implementer plus a rule that at least 50% of population-NMEC payments be tied to actual measured savings.

**The "Measured Savings" program, precisely named.** The client's reference — an implementer proposing to enroll customers, with a manufacturer bearing install cost and the implementer collecting the incentive — matches the **Market Access Program (MAP)**, also called the **Market Access Approach (MAA)** in PG&E's own CPUC-aligned "Resource Savings Rulebook." Originating in 2022 as an emergency, one-summer response to Governor Newsom's July 2021 grid-reliability proclamation (D.21-12-011), it was implemented separately by PG&E, SCE, SDG&E, and MCE (Marin Clean Energy) via their own Implementation Plans and Tier 2 Advice Letters. Structure: non-utility "aggregators" (PG&E/SDG&E terminology) or "TradePros" (SCE terminology) recruit customers, front installation cost or arrange financing, install a largely open/measure-agnostic set of measures, and are paid quarterly by the utility strictly on delivered, NMEC-verified Total System Benefit — net of a marketplace management fee (~37% of budget in PG&E's plan).

**Is it still active?** PG&E's Resource Savings Rulebook states the Market Access Approach is no longer a one-off: "All of the non-REN PAs (IOUs plus MCE) shall make available programs using the Market Access Approach... with start dates no later than July 1, 2024" — i.e., it is now a mandatory, permanent portfolio feature. However: (a) SDG&E's own "Market Access Program Aggregators" page confirms its original 2022-2023-authorized ("CORE MAP") tranche is **officially closed as of June 1, 2025** (installs due June 30, 2025) — whether a newer, mandate-driven SDG&E cycle exists was not found; (b) PG&E's and SCE's current (2025-2026) branded/live enrollment pages could not be located (the original pge.com vanity URL now redirects to a generic savings page); (c) SoCalGas is the one utility where a **live, current** third-party program roster was directly confirmed (GRID-MAP under Mendota Group LLC; Service RCx+ under Veolia North America), alongside conventional deemed-measure programs run by ICF Resources, CLEAResult, Franklin Energy, and others.

**Thresholds.** No "50,000 kWh"-type gate was found specific to the MAP/MAA aggregator model itself. The closest real analogs: SCE's separate, standing site-level NMEC "Customized" offering requires ≥100,000 kWh **and** ≥10% of the site's annual electric usage **and** a $12,000 minimum incentive (but this pays the *customer*, not an aggregator — a different, adjacent program). SoCalGas segments its commercial tiers at 50,000 therms (C-BEST vs. Large Public Sector) — a program-eligibility split, not a P4P minimum, and gas-denominated rather than electric.

**The Peak Energy question.** No CPUC or utility primary-source document (PG&E's MAP Implementation Plan or Resource Savings Rulebook, SCE's SRP Implementation Plan or NMEC Procedures Manual, SDG&E's MAP advice letter or its current Aggregators page, or SoCalGas's live third-party roster) names "Peak Energy" anywhere — **not found in primary sources.** However, a real company, **Peak Energy Solutions** (peakenergyllc.com), does exist and its own marketing site explicitly describes itself as working with "Market Access Programs in California," using "meter-based savings methods" to establish hourly savings profiles for commercial retrofits, and helping customers/contractors secure and get compensated for rebates — a description strikingly close to the client's hypothetical. SDG&E's own "Market Access Program Aggregators" page, however, does **not** publish a roster of qualified aggregators (only an application process), so Peak Energy Solutions' current qualified/registered status with any specific utility could not be independently confirmed either way. **"Tri-S" and "CryoGenX4" were not found in any CPUC or utility program material** — only on Tri-S's own commercial marketing pages, with no regulatory or utility-program nexus discovered, and no confirmed partnership between Peak Energy Solutions and Tri-S/CryoGenX4.

**Measure fit.** Both site- and population-NMEC frameworks are structurally measure-agnostic (paid on verified whole-meter/portfolio consumption change, not a deemed list), so the optimizer and CryoGenX4 are plausible fits precisely because they lack DEER/workpaper listings that would exclude them from conventional deemed-savings catalogs. RTU replacement and thermal barrier are safe fits under either pathway. Solar thermal is plausible on paper but was not specifically named as an example measure category in any document reviewed.
